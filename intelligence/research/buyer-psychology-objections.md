## 🧠 BUILD VS. BUY DECISION FRAMEWORK

### When Protocols Choose to BUILD In-House

**Research Finding:** "Buy to standardize, build to compete. If the solution is part of a core process and acts as a differentiating factor, build it."

**Red Flags (They'll Build):**
- Protocol has >10 engineers
- Analytics is core to their value prop
- They have budget for 3-6 month build
- CTO wants control over data pipeline
- Already built some internal tools

**When They Actually Build:**
1. Large protocols (>$100M TVL)
2. Data is their differentiator (like dYdX trading analytics)
3. Very specific requirements
4. Have engineering resources to spare

**Numia Counter-Strategy:**
- Target: Small-medium protocols (<50M TVL)
- Angle: "Use us to validate demand, then build if needed"
- Offer: White-label so it looks like theirs
- Frame: "Your engineers should build core product, not data pipes"

---

### When Protocols Choose to BUY

**Decision Factors (From Research):**

**1. Time to Market**
- "Buying allows teams to implement quickly and focus on core functions"
- **Trigger:** When they need analytics NOW (launch approaching, community asking)
- **Timing:** First 90 days post-mainnet = highest buying intent

**2. Cost Comparison**
- Building costs: $200K+ (3 engineers × 4 months × $50K/month)
- Ongoing maintenance: $100K+/year (1 engineer)
- Hidden costs: Opportunity cost of engineers not building core product
- **Numia Pricing:** $6K-$20K/year
- **ROI:** 10-20x cheaper than building

**3. Risk Mitigation**
- "Buying a solution involves licensing fees but can reduce development costs and speed up time to market"
- No risk of project failing
- Vendor handles maintenance, upgrades, chain updates
- **Frame:** "We de-risk your data infrastructure"

**4. Resource Constraints**
- Most Cosmos protocols have 3-10 engineers
- Can't afford to dedicate 3 to analytics
- **Trigger:** When CTO is overworked and says "we don't have bandwidth"

## 💬 OBJECTION HANDLING SCRIPTS

### Objection 1: "Why not just use Dune?"

**What They're Really Asking:**
- "Is Numia necessary if Dune exists?"
- Testing if you know your differentiation

**BAD Response:**
❌ "Dune is bad" (makes you sound desperate)
❌ "We're better than Dune" (not credible)

**GOOD Response:**
✅ "Great question. Dune is excellent for Ethereum. Problem is, they only support 2 of 90+ Cosmos chains - Sei and Noble.

For Osmosis, Celestia, dYdX, or any other Cosmos chain, Dune simply doesn't work. Their architecture was built for EVM chains.

We're built for Cosmos from day one. Think of us as Dune for the Interchain.

Which chains does your protocol need to track?"

**Key Elements:**
- Respect Dune (don't trash talk)
- State fact (only 2 chains)
- Explain why (architecture)
- Redirect (ask about their needs)

---

### Objection 2: "We can build this ourselves"

**What They're Really Asking:**
- "Is this hard to build?"
- "Can we save money by building?"
- Possibly: "I want control"

**Qualification Questions First:**
Ask these before responding:
1. "Have you started building it?"
2. "How many engineers would you dedicate?"
3. "What's your timeline?"
4. "What's your biggest data need right now?"

**If They HAVEN'T Started Building:**
✅ "I totally get that - most protocols think that initially.

Here's what we've seen: Building basic analytics takes 3-4 months with 3 engineers. That's $200K+ in engineering cost, plus another $100K/year in maintenance.

More importantly, your engineers won't be building your core product during that time.

What if you used Numia for the first 6-12 months to validate what your community actually needs, then build in-house if it makes sense? You'd know exactly what to build.

We've had 3 protocols do this - two ended up staying with us because it wasn't worth the engineering cost."

**If They HAVE Started Building:**
✅ "That's great that you've started! How's it going?

Most protocols we work with had built basic analytics but hit a wall when they needed:
- Real-time alerts
- IBC flow tracking
- Historical data going back to genesis
- Multi-chain queries

The infrastructure gets complex fast. What's your team stuck on?

We could handle the heavy lifting while your internal tools focus on your protocol-specific metrics. Many customers use us for the commoditized stuff and build only the unique parts."

**Key Elements:**
- Don't fight the objection directly
- Show the math (time, cost, opportunity cost)
- Offer hybrid approach
- Real examples (social proof)

---

### Objection 3: "Mintscan is free - why should we pay?"

**What They're Really Asking:**
- "What do you do that Mintscan doesn't?"
- "Justify your pricing"

**BAD Response:**
❌ "Mintscan sucks" (unprofessional, untrue)
❌ "We have more features" (vague)

**GOOD Response:**
✅ "Mintscan is fantastic - we use it ourselves for quick lookups. It's the best block explorer for Cosmos.

The difference is this: Mintscan is for exploring (finding transactions, checking wallets). Numia is for analyzing (understanding trends, tracking metrics, building reports).

Think of it like: Mintscan = Google Maps (find an address)
Numia = GPS analytics (where do people actually go?)

Can you pull up 'all wallets that staked >10K tokens in the last 30 days' in Mintscan? Or 'IBC flow from Osmosis to dYdX over time'? That's what Numia does.

We're actually complementary. What metrics is your community asking for?"

**Key Elements:**
- Respect Mintscan (even compliment them)
- Clear distinction (explorer vs. analytics)
- Analogy (makes it click)
- Concrete examples
- Position as complementary, not competitive

---

### Objection 4: "We don't have budget for this"

**What They're Really Asking:**
- Possibly: "This isn't a priority"
- Possibly: "We genuinely can't afford it"
- Possibly: "Show me ROI"

**Qualification Question:**
"I understand - what's your current budget situation? Have you raised funding, or are you bootstrapped?"

**If They're Pre-Funding:**
✅ "Got it. A few options:

1. We offer a free tier that covers basic analytics - might be enough for now
2. We've partnered with AADAO and other grant programs - we can help you apply for a grant that covers our services
3. If analytics helps you hit milestones for fundraising, we can defer payment until you close your round

What matters most: getting you the data you need to grow. How can we make that work?"

**If They're Funded But Tight:**
✅ "I get it - budgets are tight.

Quick question: How much would it cost if you built this in-house?

[Let them answer - usually they say 'I don't know' or lowball it]

Most protocols underestimate. It's $200K+ to build + $100K/year to maintain.

Numia is $18K/year for Team tier ($1,500/month). That's 90% cheaper than building.

Plus, if it doesn't work out, you can cancel. If you build in-house and it doesn't work, you've burned $200K.

What if we did a 3-month pilot at half price so you can see the ROI?"

**If They're Just Not Interested:**
✅ "No worries - we're not the right fit for everyone.

What would make this a priority for you? Understanding that helps us know if/when to follow up.

[Listen to their answer - this reveals true priorities]

Mind if I check in when [their trigger happens]?"

**Key Elements:**
- Don't be desperate
- Show ROI calculation
- Offer creative solutions (grants, pilots, flexible payment)
- If genuinely not interested, gracefully exit but stay in touch

---

### Objection 5: "How do we know your data is accurate?"

**What They're Really Asking:**
- "Can we trust you?"
- "What if there's an error and we make wrong decisions?"
- Testing your credibility

**GOOD Response:**
✅ "That's the most important question - you're betting your decisions on this data.

Here's how we ensure accuracy:

1. **Same source as validators:** We index directly from nodes we run. Same data validators use for consensus.

2. **Double-checked:** Cross-reference with Mintscan and other explorers. Discrepancies = we investigate.

3. **Open data:** All our dashboards are public. If we had bad data, the community would call us out immediately.

4. **Used by dYdX and Osmosis:** They're trusting us for mission-critical analytics. dYdX tracks 1M users with our data.

5. **Test it yourself:** Here's a dashboard. Cross-check 10 random transactions with Mintscan. They'll match.

We've indexed 1 billion+ transactions. If our data was off, we wouldn't be here.

What specific chain or metric are you most concerned about? Let me show you."

**Key Elements:**
- Take concern seriously (don't dismiss)
- Concrete proof (how you ensure accuracy)
- Social proof (dYdX, Osmosis)
- Offer to verify (confidence move)
- Specific examples

---

### Objection 6: "What if you shut down?"

**What They're Really Asking:**
- "What's our risk exposure?"
- "How stable are you as a company?"
- Often a proxy for "you're too small"

**GOOD Response:**
✅ "Fair concern - no one wants to depend on a tool that might disappear.

Few things on this:

1. **We're backed by Binary Builders** - they've been Cosmos validators since [year], validating on 80+ chains. We're not going anywhere.

2. **Open data:** Your data is never locked in. Export to CSV anytime. If we shut down (we won't), you keep your data.

3. **Enterprise SLA:** For Team/Enterprise tiers, we include a data export clause: if we ever shut down, we'll give you all your data plus 6 months of free service while you transition.

4. **Traction:** We're powering analytics for dYdX and Osmosis. We're growing, not shrinking.

That said, I understand the concern. What would make you comfortable?

Some customers do a 3-month trial instead of annual - that way if you're not happy or something changes, you're not locked in."

**Key Elements:**
- Acknowledge risk is real
- Show stability (Binary Builders backing)
- Show traction (existing customers)
- Offer data portability (reduces risk)
- Flexible terms (reduces commitment friction)

---

### Objection 7: "The Graph is more decentralized"

**What They're Really Asking:**
- "Isn't decentralization better?"
- "Should we use a decentralized solution?"
- Testing your understanding of Web3 values

**GOOD Response:**
✅ "The Graph is an incredible project - decentralized indexing is the future.

Two things to consider:

1. **Cosmos support:** The Graph only supports Cosmos Hub in beta. That's 1 of 90+ chains. If you need Osmosis, dYdX, Celestia, etc., it's not an option yet.

2. **Complexity:** The Graph requires developers to write subgraphs, stake $GRT tokens, manage gas fees. It's powerful but complex.

Numia is more centralized, yes - but that means it's faster to set up and simpler to use. Think of us as the bridge until The Graph fully supports Cosmos.

Some customers use both: The Graph for decentralized infrastructure, Numia for quick analytics and dashboards.

What's more important for your use case - decentralization or speed to market?"

**Key Elements:**
- Respect The Graph (don't trash decentralization)
- State facts (limited Cosmos support, complexity)
- Position as complementary, not competitive
- Ask what matters more (speed vs. decentralization)

---

### Objection 8: "We're just launching, we'll worry about analytics later"

**What They're Really Asking:**
- "Is this really important now?"
- "Can't we focus on product first?"

**BAD Response:**
❌ "You need this now!" (pushy)
❌ "Everyone else does it" (peer pressure)

**GOOD Response:**
✅ "Smart - focus on product first is the right call.

Here's when most protocols wish they had analytics:

Week 1: 'How many unique wallets interacted with us?'
Week 2: 'Where are our users coming from?'
Week 4: 'Are users coming back or churning?'
Week 8: 'Community is asking for metrics - we have nothing to show'

The problem with 'later' is you lose the early data. Can't go back in time.

What if we just set up basic tracking now (takes 15 minutes), and you can start actually using it when you're ready? Free for the first 90 days.

That way you have Day 1 data when you need it."

**Key Elements:**
- Validate their priority (product first is right)
- Paint the future pain (specific weeks)
- Show consequence (lost data)
- Low-effort solution (15 minutes, free)

## 🎯 BUYING TRIGGERS - WHEN THEY'RE READY

### High-Intent Signals

**Immediate Buying Intent (Close This Week):**
1. "Community is asking for data" = They're embarrassed, need solution NOW
2. "Investors want metrics" = External pressure, budget approved
3. "Launching in 30 days" = Urgency, checklist item
4. "We tried building it, it's harder than expected" = Pain is real
5. "Just raised funding" = Budget available, building team

**Medium-Term Intent (Close This Month):**
6. "Exploring options" = In research phase, not urgent
7. "How much does it cost?" = Price discovery, budget planning
8. "Can we try it?" = Wants proof, may be evaluating competitors
9. "Who else uses you?" = Seeking social proof, needs validation
10. "What chains do you support?" = Qualifying your product fit

**Low Intent (Nurture for Months):**
11. "Just checking it out" = Browsing, no immediate need
12. "We're pre-launch" = No users yet, no data need yet
13. "Send me info" = Polite brush-off, not engaged
14. "We'll get back to you" = Not a priority

## 🧪 BUYER PERSONAS & DECISION MAKERS

### Persona 1: Technical Founder/CTO

**Psychology:**
- Values: Control, technical depth, cost-efficiency
- Fears: Vendor lock-in, data inaccuracy, waste of money
- Decision Process: Evaluates technically first, price second

**How to Sell:**
- Lead with technical specs (API docs, data accuracy)
- Show GitHub, open schema, export capabilities
- Offer API trial before talking price
- Frame as "developer tool" not "analytics platform"

**Objections They'll Raise:**
- "Can we build this?" (Yes, but should you?)
- "How do you index data?" (Technical validation)
- "What's your uptime?" (Reliability check)

---

### Persona 2: Marketing/Growth Lead

**Psychology:**
- Values: User insights, content for community, growth metrics
- Fears: Looking bad to community, missing growth opportunities
- Decision Process: Needs buy-in from CTO, but they drive urgency

**How to Sell:**
- Lead with dashboards, visualizations, community-facing features
- Show how other protocols showcase metrics
- Frame as "community engagement tool"
- Offer white-label so it's branded as theirs

**Objections They'll Raise:**
- "Can we customize it?" (Branding matters)
- "What metrics can we show publicly?" (Need content)
- "How do other protocols use this?" (Social proof)

---

### Persona 3: Community Manager

**Psychology:**
- Values: Answering community questions, transparency
- Fears: Community backlash, not having answers
- Decision Process: Needs approval from above, but feels pain daily

**How to Sell:**
- Show how to answer common community questions
- "Track anything" positioning
- Quick setup (they're busy)
- Free tier (they may not have budget authority)

**Objections They'll Raise:**
- "Can community access this?" (Public dashboards)
- "Is it hard to set up?" (Time constraints)
- "Can I try it free?" (Budget constraints)

---

### Persona 4: Investor/Advisor

**Psychology:**
- Values: ROI, growth metrics, decision-making data
- Fears: Bad investment, missed red flags
- Decision Process: Influences protocol, doesn't buy directly

**How to Sell:**
- Position as "investor tool" (track portfolio)
- Show protocol performance benchmarks
- Frame as risk mitigation
- Offer discounts for investor referrals

**Objections They'll Raise:**
- "Can I track multiple protocols?" (Portfolio view)
- "What about competitor data?" (Benchmarking)
- "Is this investor-grade?" (Data quality)

## 💡 BUYER PSYCHOLOGY PRINCIPLES

### Principle 1: People Buy from People Like Them

**Implication for Numia:**
- Binary Builders = Cosmos validators = "One of us"
- Lean into insider status
- Use Cosmos jargon authentically
- Reference ecosystem history

**Example:**
"We've been in Cosmos since Tendermint days. We remember the pre-IBC era when cross-chain was just a dream. Now we're helping build the data layer for the Interchain."

---

### Principle 2: Social Proof > Your Claims

**Implication:**
- "Used by dYdX and Osmosis" is more powerful than any feature list
- Lead with customers, not features
- Case studies are your best sales asset

**Priority of Proof:**
1. Logo of customer's competitor ("Osmosis uses us")
2. Testimonial quote
3. Specific metrics ("dYdX tracks 1M users")
4. Number of customers ("50+ protocols")
5. Your claims about features (least persuasive)

---

### Principle 3: Loss Aversion > Gain Seeking

**What This Means:**
- People fear losing more than they want to gain
- "You're losing data" > "You'll gain insights"
- "Other protocols are ahead" > "You'll be ahead"

**Frame Numia As:**
❌ "Gain insights into your ecosystem" (weak)
✅ "Don't lose historical data you can't get back" (strong)

❌ "Track more metrics" (weak)
✅ "Stop flying blind while competitors have data" (strong)

---

### Principle 4: Smaller Commitment First

**Sales Process:**
1. Free dashboard (zero commitment)
2. Free trial (15 minutes of effort)
3. Freemium tier (zero dollars)
4. Paid tier (small investment)
5. Annual contract (big commitment)

**Don't Ask for Big Commitment Early:**
❌ "Sign annual contract" as first touchpoint
✅ "Here's a free dashboard we made for you" as first touchpoint

## 📋 QUALIFICATION CHECKLIST

**Before Spending Time, Qualify:**

✅ **Do they have users?**
- If no: Low priority (come back when they launch)
- If yes: Qualified

✅ **Do they have budget?**
- Bootstrapped: Free tier or grant funding
- Funded: Can pay, focus on value
- Enterprise: Price is less important

✅ **Do they have pain?**
- "Community asking for metrics" = High pain
- "Would be nice to have" = Low pain
- Pain = Urgency

✅ **Are we talking to decision maker?**
- Community manager: Needs CTO approval
- CTO/Founder: Can say yes
- Developer: Needs budget approval

✅ **Timeline?**
- "Need this now" = Close this week
- "Evaluating options" = Close this month
- "Just exploring" = Nurture

**If 3+ boxes checked:** High priority lead
**If 1-2 boxes:** Medium priority
**If 0 boxes:** Put in nurture sequence

## 🎯 CLOSING TECHNIQUES

### Technique 1: The Pilot Close

"How about we do a 30-day pilot? No commitment after that. You'll know in 30 days if this is valuable or not. Sound fair?"

**When to Use:** They're interested but hesitant

---

### Technique 2: The Alternative Close

"Would you rather start with the Pro tier ($149/mo) or Team tier ($499/mo)?"

(Notice: Not asking IF they'll buy, asking WHICH they'll buy)

**When to Use:** They've said yes in principle, just need to pick tier

---

### Technique 3: The Urgency Close

"We're capping new customers this quarter at 10. We have 3 spots left. Want me to reserve one for you while you decide?"

**When to Use:** They're dragging feet, need nudge
**Warning:** Only use if TRUE (don't fake scarcity)

---

### Technique 4: The Direct Close

"Based on everything we've discussed, it sounds like Numia solves your problems. Should we get you set up today?"

**When to Use:** Strong buying signals, just need to ask

---

### Technique 5: The Walkaway Close

"It sounds like this isn't the right fit for you right now. That's totally fine. Can I check back in [specific future date/trigger]?"

**When to Use:** They're clearly not ready, but you want to stay top of mind
**Benefit:** Removes pressure, makes them reconsider

---

## 🚨 RED FLAGS - WHEN TO WALK AWAY

**Don't Chase These:**

❌ **Endless tire-kickers:** 5+ meetings, still "thinking about it"
❌ **Unrealistic expectations:** "Can you build us custom X, Y, Z for free?"
❌ **Budget too small:** They want Enterprise features at Free tier price
❌ **Decision paralysis:** Can't make any decision about anything
❌ **Bad fit:** They need something Numia genuinely doesn't do

**Your Time is Valuable:**
- Focus on qualified leads
- Politely exit bad-fit conversations
- Put tire-kickers in automated nurture
