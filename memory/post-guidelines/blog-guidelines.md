# Blog Post Guidelines

## Avoiding AI-Sounding Content

### 5 Ways to Spot AI-Generated Content (NEVER DO THIS):
- Repetitive phrasing and overused connectors
- Overuse of generic transitions like "Moreover," "Furthermore"
- Lack of storytelling or emotion
- Over-explaining obvious concepts
- Flat, predictable tone

### 17 Ways to Humanize Your Writing:
- Add personal anecdotes and human examples
- Vary sentence structure and rhythm
- Use rhetorical questions and informal transitions
- Include opinions and bold statements
- Break grammar rules strategically
- Use contractions and casual language
- Add humor, metaphors, and emotions
- Adjust content to your unique style
- Refer to current events
- Treat AI as a rough draft
- Provide reliable data
- Use active voice
- Fact-check the content
- Be concise and delete fluff
- Use first-person pronouns
- Improve your AI prompts
- Check your text with AI detectors

## Blog Structure

- **Hook** (first 2 sentences): Create curiosity or state a bold claim
- **Subheadings**: Make them interesting, not generic ("The Hidden Cost of Data Lag" not "Introduction")
- **Body**: Include specific examples, numbers, mini-stories, real scenarios
- **Conclusion**: Clear takeaway or call to action
- **Length**: 800-1500 words for most topics

## Blog Writing Tips

- Sound like a peer who's been there, not a vendor
- Use contractions and vary sentence length
- Start sentences with "And" or "But" when it flows naturally
- Include conversational phrases: "Here's the thing," "Look," "Real talk"
- Show with specific examples, not vague statements
- Every sentence must add value
- Cut all corporate jargon and AI clichés
- Speak directly to "you"

## Blog Subheading Formula

Instead of boring headers, use:

- **Questions**: "Why Does Every Provider Lag at the Worst Possible Time?"
- **Bold Claims**: "Multi-Provider Setups Are Making Your Life Harder, Not Easier"
- **Specific Scenarios**: "The 3am Alert That Could've Been Prevented"
- **Contrarian**: "Stop Optimizing Your RPC Calls (Do This Instead)"

## Blog Opening Hooks (Choose One Type)

**The Scenario Hook**: "It's 2am. Your phone buzzes. The dashboard is down again. You're cycling through three different provider status pages trying to figure out which one failed this time."

**The Bold Claim Hook**: "Most blockchain developers are using the wrong infrastructure stack. Not because they're inexperienced, but because the market's been lying to them about what 'reliability' actually means."

**The Question Hook**: "How many times have you refreshed your dashboard this week because the numbers didn't look right? If it's more than once, your data provider is costing you more than their monthly fee."

**The Stat Hook**: "According to our analysis of 847 production dApps, the average team spends 11.3 hours per week debugging data provider issues. That's 589 hours per year. Per team."

## Technical Content Guidelines

When writing technical content:

- **Code examples**: Use real, working code - not pseudocode
- **Architecture diagrams**: Describe them in detail if including
- **Performance metrics**: Always include context (e.g., "2 second response time under 10k requests/min")
- **Comparisons**: Be specific and fair (never trash competitors by name)
- **API examples**: Show actual request/response, not just theory

### Good Technical Writing:

```
Here's what the query looks like:

GET /v1/balances?address=0x... &chain=ethereum

You'll get back:
{
  "balance": "1.247 ETH",
  "timestamp": 1634567890,
  "block_height": 13241234
}

The timestamp tells you exactly when that balance was accurate. If it's older than 30 seconds, you know you're looking at stale data.
```

### Bad Technical Writing:

"Our API provides balance information with timestamp data for staleness detection across multiple chains."

## Good vs Bad Examples

### Blog Introduction

**❌ Bad**: "In today's rapidly evolving blockchain landscape, data infrastructure plays a critical role in enabling developers to build robust, scalable applications."

**✅ Good**: "Here's what no one tells you about blockchain data providers: they're all fast enough to be frustrating. 30 seconds of lag isn't enough to timeout, but it's enough to make your users think your app is broken."

The Core Philosophy
AI is your writing sidekick, not the writer. It handles the heavy lifting (structure, first draft, outline) while you add the personal touches that make it genuinely connect with readers.
Think of it like power tools: Nobody questions your house because builders used power tools. Nobody should question your content because you used AI efficiently.
The goal: Work smarter, not harder, while staying true to your voice. Use AI as a foundation, never a finish line. Your humanity isn't just a differentiator - it's your superpower.

Final Reminder for Every Post
Before publishing, ask yourself:

Did I read this out loud?
Does this sound like ME?
Did I break at least 2-3 grammar rules?
Are there specific details only I would know?
Would I actually say this to a developer friend?

If you answered "no" to any of these, go back and humanize it more.
