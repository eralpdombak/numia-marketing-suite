## Content Writing Principles

### Sound Human, Not AI (CRITICAL)

This is your #1 priority. Everything else flows from this.

**How to Write Like a Human**

Use contractions religiously:
- don't, we'll, it's, you're, can't, won't, they've

Vary sentence length dramatically:
- Mix short, punchy sentences with longer, flowing ones that explore an idea fully
- Example: "Your dashboard lies. Not sometimes—constantly. Every time you refresh and see a different number, that's not your code acting up, it's your data provider serving you stale information from three different sources that can't agree on what's real."

Include intentional imperfections:
- Start sentences with "And" or "But" when it flows naturally
- Use fragments for emphasis. Like this.
- End sentences with prepositions if that's how people talk
- Don't obsess over perfect grammar

Use conversational phrases:
- "Here's the thing,"
- "Look,"
- "Real talk,"
- "The truth is"
- "Here's what nobody tells you:"
- "And this is where it gets worse:"

Add personality:
- Opinions (not everything needs to be neutral)
- Light humor (if it's natural, not forced)
- Relatable examples from real scenarios
- Frustration, excitement, empathy—show emotion

### The Humanity Injection Checklist

Every piece of content should have:
- ✅ Specific details only a human would know
- ✅ At least one opinion or hot take
- ✅ Varied sentence structure (not all same length)
- ✅ Contractions throughout
- ✅ At least 2-3 deliberately broken grammar rules
- ✅ Conversational phrases that feel natural
- ✅ Emotion (frustration, relief, excitement)
- ✅ Stories or scenarios, not just concepts

### 5 Ways to Spot AI-Generated Content (NEVER DO THIS)

If your content has these, rewrite it:
1. Repetitive phrasing and overused connectors ("Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally")
2. Overuse of generic transitions that sound academic
3. Lack of storytelling or emotion - just facts with no feeling
4. Over-explaining obvious concepts - treating readers like they're children
5. Flat, predictable tone - sounds like it could be about anything

### 17 Ways to Humanize Your Writing

Use these techniques in every piece:

1. Add personal anecdotes and human examples - "Talked to 3 teams this week who..."
2. Vary sentence structure and rhythm - Long, flowing sentences. Then short ones.
3. Use rhetorical questions - "Sound familiar?"
4. Include informal transitions - "And here's the thing—"
5. Break grammar rules strategically - Fragments. Run-ons when they work.
6. Use contractions and casual language - Always
7. Add humor, metaphors, and emotions - When authentic
8. Adjust content to your unique style - Sound like Numia, not Generic Tech Company
9. Refer to current events - When relevant
10. Treat AI as a rough draft - Never as the final output
11. Provide reliable data - Verify everything
12. Use active voice - "We built" not "It was built"
13. Fact-check the content - Never trust AI stats
14. Be concise and delete fluff - Cut 30% of what you write
15. Use first-person pronouns - "We" not "Numia," "I" when appropriate
16. Improve your AI prompts - Get better raw material
17. Check your text with AI detectors - But trust your human ear more

## Show, Don't Tell (Critical Writing Principle)

### The Rule
Use specific examples instead of vague statements. Every. Single. Time.

### Examples

❌ **Vague (AI-sounding):**
"Numia provides reliable data infrastructure for blockchain developers."

✅ **Specific (Human):**
"Your dashboard shows 1.3 ETH. Etherscan shows 1.7 ETH. You refresh four times. With Numia, both numbers match because we're pulling from the same source—always."

❌ **Vague:**
"Our infrastructure is fast and reliable."

✅ **Specific:**
"2-second response time under 10k requests/min. 99.99% uptime last quarter. No 3am alerts."

❌ **Vague:**
"We support multiple chains."

✅ **Specific:**
"100+ chains. Same API call whether you're querying Ethereum or Cosmos. No new docs to read."

### The "Would I Say This?" Test

Before publishing any sentence, ask:
1. Would I actually say this out loud to a developer friend?
2. Is this specific enough that only someone who's actually experienced this would write it?
3. Does this teach something or just take up space?

If you wouldn't say it, rewrite it.

## Content Structure Principles

### Concise and Direct

Every piece of content must do at least one of these:
- Make a specific point clearly
- Teach something actionable
- Entertain or inspire

If it does none of these, delete it.

### Front-Load Value

The first 2 sentences must:
- State a clear benefit or insight
- Hook with a specific problem or scenario
- Make them want to keep reading

Never start with:
- Context or setup
- "In today's..."
- Introducing yourself or the company
- Explaining what you're about to say

### Cut the Fluff

Delete these immediately:
- "It's important to note that..."
- "As you may know..."
- "In order to..."
- "The fact of the matter is..."
- "At this point in time..."
- Any sentence that could be cut without losing meaning

**The 30% Rule:** After writing, cut 30% of the words. Your content will improve.

### Use "You" Constantly

Speak directly to the reader:
- ✅ "You're checking three different status pages"
- ❌ "Developers often check multiple status pages"

## Writing Process (How to Actually Do This)

### Step 1: Get the Raw Draft

If using AI:
- Give it detailed prompts with examples
- Treat the output as a rough outline only
- Never publish AI output directly

### Step 2: Humanize It

1. Read out loud - Does it sound like you?
2. Add specifics - Replace every vague statement
3. Break rules - Add fragments, start with And/But
4. Inject personality - Opinions, emotion, humor
5. Vary rhythm - Mix long and short sentences
6. Add contractions - Every single "do not" becomes "don't"
7. Cut 30% - Be ruthless
8. Remove AI tells - Check the banned words list
9. Add scenarios - Replace concepts with stories
10. Test the opener - Would this hook you?

### Step 3: The Quality Checklist

Before publishing, verify:
- ☐ Would I actually say this out loud?
- ☐ Is there a clear point/benefit in the first 2 sentences?
- ☐ Did I cut all corporate jargon and AI clichés?
- ☐ Are there specific examples (not vague concepts)?
- ☐ Does it sound like a human with personality wrote it?
- ☐ Would someone actually want to read this?
- ☐ Is every word necessary?
- ☐ Did I avoid all words from the "AI tells" list?
- ☐ Did I use "you" to speak directly to the reader?
- ☐ Are there at least 3 contractions?
- ☐ Did I break at least 2 grammar rules naturally?
- ☐ Is there emotion or personality?

If any answer is NO, fix that part.
